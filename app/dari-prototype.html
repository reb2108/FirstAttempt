Shipping it to both stores

One codebase reaches iPhone and Android. React Native with Expo is the default choice for a product this shape: the screens above are ordinary lists, forms and a confirmation flow, with no heavy graphics or platform-specific hardware use. Expo builds both binaries in the cloud, so you never need a Mac to ship to the App Store, which matters when you are working remotely.

The platform toggle above shows what actually differs. It is not the code, it is the chrome: iOS centres the screen title and labels its tab bar, Android left-aligns the title and puts a pill behind the active tab icon, and corner radii differ. React Native handles most of this through its own primitives and a handful of Platform.select calls.

What sits behind the app

Three integrations carry the whole product. Fatourati is Morocco's shared bill-payment network, run by the national card processor and connected to 32 banks and payment providers across more than 70 channels. It had handled over 240 million payments and 220 billion dirhams by the end of 2025, and it exposes a REST API. It covers utilities and school fees, which is why school fees appear in the prototype.

Mobile top-ups are the easy part: commercial airtime APIs already cover Maroc Telecom, Orange and inwi, the three operators splitting the market 43, 34 and 22 percent. Cash pickup is the fallback for anything not on the network, such as the pharmacy account in the prototype.

The one thing to settle before writing code

Fatourati's API is documented for billers publishing invoices, and payments are collected through its Moroccan bank and wallet channels. Whether a foreign payment institution can join as a paying channel is the question the whole product rests on, and I could not confirm it from public sources. Establish that first, with the processor directly. If the answer is no, the route in is partnering with a Moroccan licensed institution that is already a channel, which changes the structure but not the app.

Also worth knowing

An incumbent already sells diaspora utility payments for Morocco, covering the national utility and the Casablanca distributor, taking international cards and PayPal. It is small and the experience is thin, which is the opening. But it means the idea is validated and you are not first.
